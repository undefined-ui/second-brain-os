# What a second brain actually is

One local folder of plain text that an agent reads and writes, holding what you
have read and understood, that you can ask questions.

That is the whole definition. Everything else in this guide is detail about
making it work.

## What it is not

**Not a prettier notes app.** The point is not organisation. You can organise
notes beautifully and still never reopen them.

**Not a chatbot with your PDFs attached.** Attaching documents to a chat gets you
answers about those documents in that session. Nothing accumulates, nothing
connects, and next week you attach them again.

**Not a memory feature.** Assistants that remember facts about you are useful and
are a different thing. This is a body of knowledge you own, that outlives any
particular tool.

## The three jobs

**Capture.** Everything you read, watch and write down lands in one place with
near-zero friction. If capture takes effort, nothing else matters, because there
will be nothing to organise.

**Connect.** New material gets linked into what is already there. This is the job
humans stop doing after two weeks and the reason every previous note system
failed. It is also the entire value: unconnected notes are a folder.

**Answer.** You ask questions across everything at once, and get answers grounded
in your own sources with citations you can open.

## Where the value comes from

Not from any single page. A page is a slightly better version of a note you could
have written.

It comes from density. At fifty sources, the vault starts connecting things you
read months apart. At five hundred, it answers questions you could not have
answered yourself, because nobody holds five hundred sources in their head at
once.

That is why the first two weeks feel underwhelming and why most people quit
before the payoff. The system is not broken at thirty pages, it is just
early. See [what good looks like](what-good-looks-like.md) for a realistic
timeline.
