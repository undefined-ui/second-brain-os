# The four page types

Four types, each with a job. Every page declares its type in frontmatter, which
is what lets you query the vault by shape rather than reading everything.

## Sources

One page per ingested item. What this article, video, paper or conversation
said.

Records claims as claims: this source says X, with the URL, author and date
attached. It does not evaluate. It does not merge in what other sources think.

Source pages are the audit trail. When a concept page says something surprising,
the source pages behind it are how you check whether it was ever true.

## Entities

People, organisations, products, tools. What it is, why it appears in your
vault, and everything that mentions it.

Entity pages are cheap to create and pay off in a way that is not obvious until
the vault is large. They become the natural join points: three unrelated sources
that all mention the same company suddenly connect through it, and that
connection is often the thing worth noticing.

Do not write biographies. An entity page holds what is relevant to your vault,
not what Wikipedia holds.

## Concepts

Ideas, methods, frameworks, arguments. These are the pages that compound.

A concept page explains the idea in plain language, says where it came from,
what supports it, what argues against it, and what is still unclear. Written
well, it is readable by someone who has seen none of your sources, including
you in a year.

This is the only page type where the agent should be synthesising across
sources rather than recording.

## Synthesis

Comparisons, themes, open questions. A synthesis page exists only when it says
something no single source did: two sources disagree, three describe the same
pattern, a question keeps recurring across unrelated material.

The rule that keeps these honest: do not write a synthesis page to have one. An
agent asked for synthesis will always produce something, and manufactured themes
are the fastest way to fill a wiki with confident nonsense.

## Where the types blur

Some things are legitimately two types. A methodology named after its author is
both an entity and a concept. Pick one, usually the concept, and give the other
an alias pointing at it.

Some things are neither. A recipe, a config snippet, a checklist. Those are
reference material, not knowledge, and they belong in a project folder rather
than the wiki.
