# Learning from your own vault

Reading something is not learning it. A vault full of well-written pages you
never revisit is a library, and libraries do not make anyone knowledgeable.

## Questions from your own pages

```
Generate ten questions from my concept pages on prediction markets. Mix recall
and application. Do not include the answers.
```

Answer them without looking. The ones you cannot answer are things you saved and
never actually learned, which is most of what anyone saves.

This is more useful than generic quizzing because the questions come from
material you chose, at the depth you recorded it.

## Spaced repetition, without the card-writing

The reason spaced repetition has low adoption is that writing cards is tedious.
The vault removes that step: concept pages are the source material, and the agent
can generate cards from them.

The [Spaced Repetition](https://github.com/st3v3nmw/obsidian-spaced-repetition)
plugin reads cards written inside your notes, so they live alongside the concept
rather than in a separate app that drifts out of sync.

Keep it to a small number of pages that actually matter. Reviewing everything you
ever saved is a job, not a habit.

## Find what you saved and did not understand

```
Which of my concept pages are written in language copied from the source rather
than explained in my own words? Those are the ones I probably did not understand.
```

A page that reproduces the source's phrasing is a page nobody digested. That is a
real signal, and it is invisible until you ask.

## Explain it back

The oldest test there is:

```
Ask me to explain [[GraphRAG]] as if to someone who has not read about it. Then
compare what I said to my page and tell me what I left out.
```

Gaps between what you can say and what your page contains are precise, and they
are exactly the thing rereading never reveals.

## Measure learning, not collection

Page count goes up whether or not you know more. What is worth tracking is
whether you can answer questions from your own material without opening it.

Once a quarter is enough. The result reliably surprises people, in both
directions.
